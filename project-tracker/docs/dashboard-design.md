# Dashboard design

The spec the dashboards' code is built and reviewed against — for whoever changes `skills/project-tracker/assets/` (`dashboard.html`, `client.html`, `app.js`, `core.js`, `core.css`). The skill does not read it at run time: it only publishes and updates the page (`skills/project-tracker/references/dashboard.md`) and writes client snapshots (`references/client-dashboards.md`).

`assets/dashboard.html` is a calm, read-only overview of the tracker: it reads Notion with the viewer's own connector and computes everything else in the browser. Its code and look live in `assets/app.js`, `assets/core.js` and `assets/core.css`, which the client dashboards (`references/client-dashboards.md`) run too, on a snapshot instead of Notion; a change there shows on both. Notion stays the source of truth; the chat stays the only way to change anything, because the rules in SKILL.md (dates move only by agreement, history lines, was → now) live there. The dashboard is for the user alone.

## Who uses it, when — the scenarios

The user runs a few projects at once — Upwork contracts, direct clients, their own. The page is built from these moments; anything that serves none of them does not belong on it.

| # | Moment | Question | Answered by |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Morning glance, 10 s | Is anything on fire, what is on me? | the **pills** in the header, **Your move** |
| 2 | How is each project, overall | State, what is happening now, how far along, when it ends | a **card** per project |
| 3 | Sitting down to work on a project / before a client call | Where did I stop, what is next, what is open, what did we agree? | the **project page** that opens from the card: Now / Current task / Next / Blocked, open items, the plan — milestones with their tasks and each one's history — notes |
| 4 | Chasing | Whom do I write to, how long have they had it? | **Waiting on others** |
| 5 | Taking new work | Where are projects in time, where do they collide? | **Timeline** |
| 6 | Friday | What got done? | **Recently completed** |

## Criteria it is checked against

- **Opened, read in seconds.** One screen on a laptop for a normal load (up to ~4 projects): pills in the header; the timeline (the overview of everything) and the project cards under it (each project in more detail) on the left; Your move, Waiting on others, Risks, Recently completed on the right. No tabs; the only menus are the origin filter (with more than three origins) and the Claude button's ▾. Phones stack: the header as rows — the name with Refresh and Notion, the live line under it, the origin filter and the pills each a full row shared evenly — then Your move, timeline, cards, Waiting, Risks, Recently completed.
- **Calm.** One typeface; colour only where something needs attention (red: blocked or overdue; amber: due soon, within 7 days); one coloured signal per item; few boxes, no boxes inside boxes on the overview; short phrases in plain words, the words other trackers use (Overdue, Due soon, Recently completed).
- **Details one click away, never in Notion.** A card, a project name, a bar or any list item opens that project as a page sliding in from the right, scrolled to the item that was clicked and with it open. Items there open in place, showing their page's parts (**A page**, below) with the item's problems after its description. Every opened thing links to Notion for sharing or editing. ↑ / ↓ (and the arrows in the drawer's bar) step to the previous / next project; Esc closes an open menu first, then the page. A widen button in the bar makes the page as wide as the main page (never wider than the window), and the choice is remembered. **The bar names the project, statically** — its name and place (`Smart Feeder · 1 of 3`), ↻, widen, ↑ / ↓ and close — and has no item's buttons. **Every button sits on what it acts on**: the project's own Claude and Notion beside who it is for at the top of its page, and an opened milestone's, task's or problem's in its own header strip; a closed item has none. Nothing guesses what the reader is at.
- **An opened item's header sticks while its item is read.** **An item's header is the same folded, opened and stuck** — same lines, same height, nothing moves: a quiet meta line — its ID, its dates, its Claude control (the chat, `</>` its Code session, ▾ a new chat or session — one control, as the project's) and `Notion`, and the fold chevron, all on that one line, centred on the control — then its name, a size larger (a milestone's 15.5px semibold, a task's regular), then its line (`in progress · 18 d left · 0 of 3 tasks done`). The buttons have their place in every row but show only when the item is opened — never under the pointer, where they would catch the click that opens it. On a phone they are the marks alone (no `</>`: a Code session lives on the Mac) and the name has its line's full width. Scrolling through the item, the strip sticks to the top of the page, over the text that scrolls beneath it (a hairline under it), and goes with the item's end. **One strip is stuck at a time: the innermost opened item being read** (`stuckOne` in `core.js`). Reading an opened milestone, its header is stuck; an opened task inside it, its header rising to the top, pushes the milestone's up ahead of it and takes its place; when the task's text ends, its header is pushed out by that end and the milestone's comes back right under it, rising with it to the top, for the rest of the milestone; when the milestone ends, nothing is stuck. Two headers never overlap (`stickPlan` in `core.js`). So the stuck header is one header high (about 84 px, a long name taking its second line). An item under Open items or Resolved lately is drawn alone there, so its strip sticks by itself, whatever milestone it belongs to in the plan.
- **A fold never moves the page.** Folding an item — from its header, stuck or not, or the keyboard — or a section or sub-page keeps its header where the reader sees it: in its place, it stays put; stuck at the top while the item was read, the page moves up at once so the folded item's start is where the strip was — a task just under its milestone's strip, which sticks again, a milestone at the top. Near the page's end the page keeps room below it so the browser does not pull it back; the room goes once it is scrolled out of view. A repaint while a menu is open waits for it to close, and keyboard focus stays on what it was on.
- Every project's state is said the same way everywhere: `Blocked`, `3 d late`, a due within 7 days (the same rule as the pills and Your move) said as whose it is — `Due today` / `Due in 5 d` only for the project's own `Target end`, and for a milestone or task inside it that ends first `Milestone due today` / `Task due in 2 d` (on the project page also by name: `Task due in 2 d: Landing copy and 1 more`), the nearest winning and the project's end on a tie, since a task's end read as the project's deadline, most of all on a project with no end date — `On track`, `No dates`, `Paused`, `All done`, `No plan yet`, `Not loaded`, and `Problems unknown` when problems did not load (never `On track` on unread blockers) — from milestones and tasks alike.
- Nothing moves on its own: placeholders of the same size until the first full set of data, then updates in place (scroll, open items, fetched page text kept; a refresh never blanks).
- Honest when part of it fails: a pill shows `?` or `n+` for counts built on data that did not load, the card says what did not load, and no count is stated as zero for data that was not read.
- Nothing is hidden because it is zero: the three pills are always there, grey at zero.
- Light and dark; phone to wide desktop.
- **Same family as the Upwork Pulse dashboard**, so the user's tools look like one set. Shared, and changed in both pages together — the colour and shadow tokens have the same names in both (`--text`, `--text-2`, `--text-mute`, `--border`, `--critical`, …, `--shadow-drawer`, `--shadow-pop`, `--shadow-float`), so a shared change is a copy; no colour or shadow is written outside them (`--shadow-lift`, the timeline's lift under the cursor, is the tracker's own): system font (no web fonts), 14px base; teal accent and green-grey neutrals; 10px cards with a thin border and no shadow; a section's title sits above its card with the count right after the title and ⓘ (if any) right after that, controls pushed right; header pills (a fixed set, coloured only when not zero); segmented tabs; list rows with a 3px rounded mark inset 10px from the edge, text from 22px, a line between rows, 14.5px titles and 13px secondary text; group labels as grey uppercase bands; the header's status group — `Live · updated hh:mm`, then its buttons, 12px apart; a footer line; menus as one popover (10px radius, 6px padding, 14px rows); the drawer — 680px, tinted head with a 26px title, plaques fully tinted with a border (never a coloured side stripe), foldable sections with an icon, Back to top, ↑ / ↓ (the arrow buttons, no key-hint text), widen to the page width; drawer facts as small uppercase labels over values; the Claude button — its chat part, ▾ with New chat (the tracker's also has a Code part and the menu's Code group, its own: Pulse has no Code sessions). Page width may differ: the tracker is 1440px for the timeline, Pulse 1180px.

## Structure

- **Header**: name, origin filter, only when there is more than one kind — up to three origins as a segmented control like the timeline's zoom (`All 2 · Upwork 1 · Direct 1`), all in view and one click each; more than three as a dropdown (`All projects 5 ▾`, then each origin with its count), since the user may add any number of options; choosing one closes the menu and repaints at once — three pills — `blocked` · `overdue` · `due soon` — always shown, coloured when not zero, a click scrolls to Your move; updated time, refresh, Notion. How many things are on the user and whom they wait on are the counts next to Your move and Waiting on others, not pills.
- **Project cards**, sorted failed → blocked → late → nearest due, paused last: name, client · origin, state pill; the milestone in focus (Now / Overdue / Next / Paused) with its timing and, under it, its current task with its timing (a project without milestones shows its task in focus instead); a blocker if any; a strip of all milestones (or tasks, without milestones); how many are done, end date, how many items are on the user and waiting.
- **Timeline** (above the cards, so it stays in view however many projects there are): a row per project, bars for milestones with today marked, zoom 1 month / 3 months / 6 months / year / all (the choice is remembered), Today, a quiet grid under it — dotted days and dashed weeks (Mondays numbered) at 1 month, with weekends a faint band always darker than workdays in both themes (sunken, never lighter), dashed weeks at 3 months, months always; months are short names and January shows only its year, in bolder type, never `Jan 2027`; a week line close to a month line is left out, and so are labels that would collide; drag or scroll in time, legend behind ⓘ drawn with the timeline's own cards and chips; a hover tip on a card, bar or chip gives its state and dates (`In progress · Oct 1 – Nov 9 · 33 d left`, `Waiting on Gilad · no dates`) — not its name, which is on it; a strip segment's tip starts with the name; under the cursor (or keyboard focus) a box, bar or chip widens to show its whole name and lifts above its neighbours, so even a two-day task is readable and easy to click. Nothing on the timeline has an outline, and every fill is opaque, so a widened box or chip covers its neighbours cleanly: a milestone is a card over its dates, one even fill of fine diagonal stripes in its state's tint (in progress, late, done; grey when planned; muted with ❚❚ in place of ◆ when paused) with ◆ and its name on top, kept in view when the card starts left of the visible part; under the header its tasks — a segmented line within the milestone's dates when closed (accent in progress, red late, amber waiting, green done), flat named chips, coloured only when in progress, late, waiting or done, when ▸ next to the project opens it up (each milestone on its own line, its name also in the left column; the choice is remembered). A dated chip spans its dates; a name that does not fit in it is never cut: it goes on a faint plaque joined to the chip's end, tinted by the chip's state, that starts with a short leader (`– Demo firmware`) — one thing with the chip, yet lighter, so it never reads as more of its dates. The plaque always follows the chip, so the names read down the card in date order, and near the card's end it runs past the card into the line's empty space; lines are packed by what is drawn, plaque included, with a clear gap before the next chip. A milestone without dates is a faint grey card with a barely visible ? after its name: drawn over its dated tasks, or else as a one-month placeholder in the plan's order: after the milestone before it ends (a dated one or the placeholder before it), today at the earliest — so the one after the milestone in progress starts when that one ends. Tasks never carry ◆, so header and task can't be confused. A milestone or task whose `Dates` has a start and no end (an end not agreed yet — the skill never writes one, the user may type one in Notion) is drawn from its start for the placeholder's month, fading out on the right (one rule: an edge that is no date fades — an undated milestone's placeholder fades at both ends), and reads `started Oct 8 · no end date`, `from Oct 8` for its dates; it is never late or due. Tasks without dates are normal — only a milestone's dates are promised — so a milestone's undated tasks sit in its card after the dated ones and are drawn like any other task: closed, as one line of marks sharing the card evenly; opened, as chips each as wide as its name, as many to a line as fit and the rest on more lines, so the card grows down, not sideways (only a name wider than the whole card is cut, and it shows whole under the cursor). Under the cursor a card widens only to fit its own name. An undated milestone's placeholder spans a month, never narrower than one chip; with no `?` (that mark is for an undated milestone only); a milestone with any tasks gets the ▸. A project without milestones draws its dated tasks as the bars; a task with neither a milestone nor dates is not drawn. The left column gives a project's name up to two lines, its row growing to fit. A phone has no left column: each project's name, with its ▸ and dot, is a line of its own above its bars, which get the screen's whole width; milestone names then live on their cards only.
- **↻ in the project page's top bar**, the same as Upwork Pulse's drawer: reads everything again, like the header's Refresh, while the project page stays open and repaints with what changed; its arrow turns until the load that reads it again ends, however long that takes, as the header's Refresh (↻ and the word) does. On a phone the bar keeps its buttons and the project's name on one line.
- **Claude**, one split button, `[ <chat mark> Claude ↗ | </> | ▾ ]`, with `Notion` after it, sits on a meta line: the project's on its head's (below), a milestone's, task's or problem's on its header's (above) — the same control, the same size, everywhere. Near the page's bottom edge a menu opens upwards. Each row has two threads, side by side (`references/chats.md`): a Cowork **chat**, its link kept in the row's `Chat`, and a Claude **Code session** on the Mac, its link kept in `Code session`; each is written by the thread itself, never by the page. The marks are the Claude app's own for its two modes — outlines in the shape of Tabler's `messages` and `code`, inline SVG in the text's colour, 15 px on the button, 13 px beside the menu's group labels; no emoji, no icon font, no dot or other sign of what is saved (the tooltip says it).
  - **The main part** opens the chat. `Chat` holds `https://claude.ai/code/session_<id>`; the button opens it on a phone as `https://claude.ai/cowork/cse_<id>`, elsewhere as `claude://claude.ai/code/session_<id>` (tooltip `Opens this task's chat`). No `Chat` yet → a new Cowork chat (`Start a chat about this task; every later click opens the same chat`) whose first message is `TSK-34 <name> — <project> (Project Tracker, <page url>). Chat title: "TSK-34 <name> — <project>". Load it with the project-tracker skill.` (the row's ID first — its `Ref` with the config's prefix, which also says its kind — then its name; a project has no project after it: `PRJ-4 <name>`; the chat's name given, since the app's own title is a guess and the chat renames itself where it can; the skill named, so the chat loads it; only what to load, no request) — `claude://cowork/new?q=…`, on a phone `https://claude.ai/new?mode=cowork&surface=cowork&project=<id>&q=…` with the project's own claude.ai project from its `Claude project` field (none → no `project`).
  - **`</>`** (about 30 px, the same tint, a thin line before it) opens the row's Code session: `Code session` holds the session's own link, `claude://claude.ai/epitaxy/local_<id>`; the page takes the `local_<id>` out of it and opens that link (`Opens this task's Code session on this Mac`). None yet → a new local Code session, `claude://code/new?folder=<the project's Code folder>&q=<first message>` (no `folder` when it is empty: the app asks for one; it always asks to confirm the folder, and the message is prefilled, sent by the user), tooltip `Start a Code session on this Mac, in ~/…; every later click opens the same session` (or `…, the app asks for the folder; …`). Its first message is the chat's with `Code session[, repository <Repository>]. ` before `Chat title`, and the same title as the chat — the words say which column the session claims; the app already marks a Code session as one, so the title needs no mark of its own.
  - **▾** holds the rare actions, by domain: a `CHAT` label with its mark, then **New chat** (with a chat: the same first message saying `Its old chat is gone: this is its chat from now on.`, for when the old one is deleted or too long, so the new one takes over the row) or **Start a chat**; a line; a `CODE` label, then **New Code session** (`Its old Code session is gone: this is its Code session from now on.`, for when the old one is archived or too long) or **Start a Code session**, and under them `Folder: ~/…` (the home folder shortened to `~`, the whole path in its tooltip, wrapping, never cut) or `Folder: not set — the app asks for it`. In a section's last rows, where the panel would clip it, the menu opens upwards when only there it fits.
  - **On a phone** a Code session cannot be reached (it lives on the Mac, and `claude://` does not open there): no `</>`, and the menu's Code group is greyed, its item saying `On the Mac`.
  - The project page's head shows the claude.ai project as a link, `open ↗`, without its name — `Claude project` keeps the name it had when it was set, a rename in claude.ai never reaches it, and nothing the page or the skill can call reads the current one (no connector or runtime capability lists claude.ai projects; a chat sees only its own project's name, and dashboard chats on the Mac open outside projects), so a shown name would go stale — or `none` in grey with how to set one in its tooltip, and the **Code folder** (`~/…`, the full path in its tooltip; read as the path alone, since Notion returns a domain-like part of it as a Markdown link and inline code as `<code>`: `plain` in `core.js`), or `not set — the first Code session fills it` in grey. A config error that a chat can fix (a 0.x tracker, missing IDs, databases or views) carries a "Fix it in a chat with Claude" link that starts one with the request.
- **One grammar for every list line** on the overview (Your move, Waiting on others, Risks, Recently completed; on a client's page its open items): first its kind's tag and its name — `Task`, `Milestone` in a quiet grey, a problem's `Blocker` / `Question` / `Risk` in its colour, the same tags as a project page — so a line never leaves the user guessing what it is — the tag hung left by its own padding, so its text, a wrapped name and the path under it all start on one line (the folded milestones without dates carry none: the line that opens them says it); under it where it sits, always the full path — `Project › Milestone › Task` as far as it goes, a problem on a task with that task's milestone too — on whom it is (**On whom**, below), then a word only where the group does not say it (`not started`, `due Oct 22`), and its ID last; on the right the one number that matters (age, date, `3 d late`). Every side panel folds, and so does every group inside one (Your move, Waiting on others, a client's Open items), with one Expand all / Collapse all in the panel's head; each viewer's choice is remembered. Everything that opens or folds — panels, groups, the project page's sections, milestones, rows, a project's line on the timeline, a toggle in a page's text — moves smoothly (height, opacity, 0.2 s), its chevron turning at once; never a jump, and none where the system asks for less motion.
- **Your move**: only what asks for an action now, each thing once, grouped by what the user does — Blocked (Blocker problems) → Overdue (milestones and tasks past their end) → Due soon (ending within 7 days) → Doing (tasks in progress with nobody to wait on) → Up next (planned tasks with nobody to wait on that start within 7 days, or have no dates under the milestone in progress or no milestone) → To decide (Questions nobody else decides) → Milestones without dates (planning); a card's `N on you` counts the lines of every group but Up next and Milestones without dates, which are not today's work — folded at first, and the timeline shows them better. An age reads `today` on its first day, and a risk shows none: it waits on no one. Risks are not a move: they have their own panel.
- **Waiting on others**: Waiting tasks and problems (not risks) with a name in `Waiting on`, by party, oldest first, with ages — a task's age counts from the start of its dates, a problem's from `Opened`.
- **Risks**: the open risks, each with the date it matters by — its task's end, else its milestone's (`by Oct 28`) — soonest first; no age, no colour bar, nothing to do but keep them in mind. No pill in the header: the pills are alarms.
- **Recently completed** (collapsed): milestones and tasks finished and problems resolved in the last 7 days.
- **Project page** (slides in from the right), built of visibly separate blocks so the eye finds each one: a tinted head that reads as an item's header, in the same order — a quiet meta line with its ID, its dates (the plan's earliest start to `Target end`: `Sep 1 – Dec 18`, `from Oct 2`, `until Dec 18`, `no dates`) and, at its right, the project's Claude control and `Notion`, the item's own size; its name; its line — the state in its colour, what is due by name when that is not the project's own end, then the project's own time (`56 d left`, `ends today`, `3 d past its end`, `no end date`); then its description, when it has one, as plain text, and under a hairline its facts in Pulse's drawer-fact style, small uppercase labels over values: Client (with the origin after it in grey, `none · Personal` without one), Source / Upwork contract when there is one, Repository when there is one, Claude project (`open ↗`, or "none" with how to set it), Code folder (the whole path, wrapped, never cut; or "not set"), last since it may take a row. No pill and no client line above the name: the state is a word on the line, as on an item, and the client is a fact like the others; the dates were two facts (Ends, Started) and are now the meta line, as on an item;  the status note as its own tinted, bordered card headed Status; a Where it stands box (Now with its current task as a "Current task:" line, Overdue, Paused, Next with "next milestone", Blocked — each a tag and a line; click jumps to the item); three number tiles — milestones done with a strip, open items, notes (public and private) — each a button that unfolds and scrolls to its section; then sections with an icon, a title and a count, each under a hairline, no panel round it, and foldable from its header (all open on every visit; an item opened from elsewhere unfolds its section) — Open items grouped as Blocking / On you / Waiting on others (Waiting tasks too) / Risks to keep in mind with a type tag on each; Resolved lately (the project's problems in the root `Recently resolved` view), so an item from Recently completed always opens in place; Plan — each milestone a foldable group: dates on the right, a short phrase (`8 d left`, `done Sep 21, on time`, `starts in 8 d`) and tasks done of total; opening it shows at once the milestone's facts, problems and page, then its tasks under a Tasks label; only the milestone under way (or late, or in focus) is open by default; with more than one milestone, the Plan head has Expand all / Collapse all for them, and a milestone with several tasks has one for its tasks on its Tasks line; tasks without a milestone last; Notes, Private notes and History — the project page's own sections. A round Back to top button appears once the page is scrolled down.

- **Every opened item is drawn the same way, at any level.** The project page draws no outline round a section or an item: rows are split by hairlines. An opened milestone, task or problem has its header in the active fill (`--sunken`) with a line under it, and its text in a region one shade off what is round it: a milestone or an open item is a rounded panel in the light tint (`--surface-2`) on the page; a task opened inside an opened milestone has its text on the page's own surface (`--surface`) inside the milestone's panel, and a line where it ends. So where a task starts and ends inside its milestone, and where the milestone goes on and ends, is plain without spacing, indents or a panel in a panel. A milestone's tasks keep the full width.
- **A page** (a milestone's, task's or problem's, opened in place; and the project's own, above) shows its parts by their headings (SKILL.md → **Pages**), never by how a line looks: the description first, unlabelled — a checklist drawn as ticked and unticked boxes, read-only — then Notes, Private notes (marked with a lock, and in a quieter tint, so what the client never sees is plain at a glance) and History, each under its label and only when it has entries; a page with none of them says "Nothing written yet." The client's page shows the same, without Private notes. A sub-page is a row that opens its text in place, with Open in Notion; an embedded database says to open the page in Notion — both read whether the fetch writes them on one line or over several (`<page url="…">`, its title, `</page>`, as Notion has since Oct 20), so no tag shows as text. A file on a page (a PDF, an image, any upload) is a chip with its name and ↗: Notion keeps it behind a reference whose download link lives a few minutes, so a click asks the connector for a fresh one (`notion-get-file-download-urls`) and opens the file in a new tab; a link from outside opens as it is. On the client's page the chip has the name only — its data keeps no source.
- **Where an item sits, and its state, in one line.** A plan row says its status where its timing does not, and a task's on whom (`in progress · 32 d left · 1 of 6 tasks done`, `in progress · on you · 13 d left`, `waiting on Lab one, Lab two · 8 d left`, `planned · no dates yet`; `done Oct 5, 4 d early` says it already) — no separate status line in its details. Wherever a problem or a waiting task is listed outside its milestone (open items, resolved lately, Your move, Waiting on others), its line names where it sits: `Demo prototype › Boards from JLCPCB`. A problem listed under a milestone or task says on whom it is, or `resolved`. The head and the cards name the client once when it is also the origin; the Notes tile counts private notes apart (`0 · 1 private`).
- **IDs and links between items.** Every project, milestone, task and problem shows its `Ref` next to its name — `PRJ-2`, `MLS-12`, `TSK-34`, `PRB-5` (the prefixes from the config's `ref_prefixes` line; the views give only the number), small, monospaced, quiet — wherever the name stands as a title (cards, lists, the project head, the plan, open items); a click copies it, to use in a chat, and does not open the row. Whatever points at another item opens it on the page — scrolled to, opened and briefly marked, on its project's page if it is another project: in a problem's line `in Demo prototype › Boards from JLCPCB`, a task's `in <milestone>`, the Problems part of a milestone or task, and a page mention in a description, note or history line. Only links that lead out of the tracker open Notion.

**Origin** comes from the Projects select `Origin`, whatever options the user added; the header's dropdown lists every value in use. A tracker without the property falls back to the rule `Source` on upwork.com → Upwork, empty `Client` → Personal, else Direct.

Rules the page keeps:

- **On whom**, the same words everywhere an open task or problem shows — the overview's lists, the cards, Where it stands, the project page's open items, plan rows and Problems parts, an opened problem's page (an opened task's row says it in its header, so its page does not again), the timeline's tips: `on you` when its `Waiting on` is empty (the next move is the user's), `waiting on <name>` when it names a party — a person, a vendor, the client, an agent such as Claude — as written (`waiting on Lab one, Lab two`). It comes first after the name and the tag: `Firmware · on you · due Nov 2`, `Lab quotes · waiting on Lab one, Lab two · due Oct 28`. Said even where the group says it too (Doing, Waiting on others), so a line read alone never leaves it open. Nothing for a milestone (it has no `Waiting on`; its tasks say it), a risk (it waits on no one), anything paused, or what is Done, Resolved or Dropped. One function (`onWhom` in `core.js`) decides the phrase for every place.

- **Order is the plan's**: milestones (and each milestone's tasks) appear everywhere — timeline rows and placeholders, the project page's Plan, the card's strip — in their `Order`, so a plan without dates keeps its sequence; rows without `Order` fall back to their dates, after the numbered ones.
- **Lateness by the same rule as the Notion formulas** (SKILL.md, *How lateness works*), for milestones and tasks alike, computed from the dates on every load against the viewer's local today. A paused milestone, its tasks, or anything in a paused project is never late. Open milestones without dates are drawn as faint placeholders in the plan's order (no earlier than today) and listed among the milestones without dates under Your move, never as due or late; a task without dates is simply undated. The page says "no dates", never "agreed dates" — every date in the tracker is agreed by definition.
- **Read-only.** No writes to Notion, no buttons that pretend to change state; the footer, as in Pulse, says changes go through the chat. Links taken from Notion fields open only as web links; `claude://` is used only by the Claude button, which builds its links itself from the session ids in `Chat` and `Code session`.
- **Views and page fetches only**, never SQL — same reason as in SKILL.md. The page discovers everything from the root page: config IDs → databases → views by name (`Active`, `Open`, `Recently resolved`) → each project page's `Schedule` and `Tasks` views. What it finds of that structure (the config's views, each project's two view ids) is kept for the session, so a poll reads only rows and pages; a Refresh finds it all again. A config with `open_items` and no `tasks` is a 0.x tracker: the page says so and points to the upgrade in chat. Page text (descriptions, notes, history, sub-pages) is fetched when a row opens and kept for the session; a closed project page reads nothing again. It is drawn from Notion's enhanced Markdown as Notion shows it — headings, lists and to-dos, quotes, callouts, toggles and toggle headings (closed), columns, tables (header row and column, colours; a wider one scrolls sideways and fades at the edge with more), code, colours, mentions — files as chips, an embedded database as a line pointing to Notion. Only the root page ID is filled in at publish.
- **Both dialects of page text.** On Oct 20, 2026 Notion's MCP changed what `notion-fetch` returns for the same pages: `<toggle>` for `<details>`/`<summary>`, `<b>`, `<i>`, `<s>`, `<code>` for `**`, `*`, `~~`, backticks, pipe tables for `<table>`, `<p />` for `<empty-block/>`, nesting by two spaces for tabs, and more escapes (`ref\_prefixes`, `09\:30`). Every page the dashboard reads (and every page text in a client's snapshot) goes once through `notionText` (core.js), which turns the new dialect into the old one; old text passes unchanged, since other workspaces may still get it. Every reader below it — the config, a page's parts, history and pause lines, counts, the Markdown renderer — reads one dialect. Backslash escapes stay in the text; `unesc` undoes any of them for display, and the config is read with them undone. `tests/check_notion_text.py` holds one fictional page per kind in both dialects.
- **Freshness and failures.** One load at a time: a Refresh or a poll (every 10 minutes, and on coming back to the tab after that) that comes during a load is never dropped — one more load starts when the current one ends, past the connector cache if a Refresh asked for it, and a Refresh is done when that load ends. "Updated hh:mm" is the cache time of the oldest row or page the last load read, so it never claims more than was read. A part that was read before and fails on a later load — the project list, a project's plan, problems, resolved problems — keeps what it showed, marked "not updated" with the reason where it shows, and the live line counts it (`Live · updated 14:05 · 1 not updated`, which ones in its tooltip). What did not load is marked on the pills and named on its card, a failed project keeps its card and timeline row and goes first, a call Notion does not answer within 45 s counts as failed (so the page never stays on Loading), each failure shows the fix for its error code, and "nothing late" is never claimed for data that was not read.

## The client's page

`assets/client.html` is the user's dashboard run on a snapshot of the projects it covers: it runs the same code (`assets/app.js`, with `assets/core.js` and `assets/core.css`) on the `data.js` the skill writes and republishes after every change (`skills/project-tracker/references/client-dashboards.md`), so the client needs no Notion access and no connector; published without its `data.js`, the page says it has no data yet instead of staying blank. Lateness is computed in the browser against the viewer's today, so it stays right between updates. One change from the user's page: a single list of open items by whose side they are on — the client's, ours, third parties' — in place of Your move and Waiting on others; blockers and questions nobody else has to act on show as "On our side", risks apart. Each open task and problem says its side in the same words wherever it shows (**On whom**): `on our side` for an empty `Waiting on`, `on your side` for a party on the client's side (`Side` `client`), `with <name>` for a third party (`with Lab one, Lab two`).
